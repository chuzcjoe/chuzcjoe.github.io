Dividend and its derived concepts.

<!-- more -->


A dividend is a portion of a company's profit that is distributed to its shareholders as a reward of their investment. Companies usually pay dividends in the forms of cash or additional shares of stock. There is another special dividend which is a one-time payment, often issued when the company has excess profits.

There are two key metrics related to dividend: `dividend yield` and `payout ratio`.

**Dividend Yield** – Measures how much a company pays in dividends relative to its stock price.

$$
\begin{equation}
\text{Dividend Yield} = \frac{\text{annual dividend per share}}{\text{curret stock price}} * 100%
\end{equation}
$$

If a company pays a `$2` annual dividend and its stock price is `$40`, this means the investor earns a `5%` return from dividends annually.

**Payout Ratio** – Shows how much of a company’s earnings are used for dividend payments.

$$
\begin{equation}
\text{Payout Ratio} = \frac{\text{total dividends}}{\text{net income}} * 100%
\end{equation}
$$

If a company earns `$10` billion in profit and pays `$4` billion in dividends, this means the company keeps `60%` of its earnings for reinvestment and other uses.

Companies usually announce dividends (quarterly or annually) as a fixed amount per share. Dividend yield fluctuates because it depends on the stock price, which changes daily.
